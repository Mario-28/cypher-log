# CYPHER LOG 📜✨
*The Journal Your Character Actually Deserves*

---

## "Dear Diary, Today I Didn't Die..."

**CYPHER LOG** is a floating, book-like journal library for Foundry VTT that makes your players actually *want* to take notes. No more "uh, what was the name of that NPC again?" moments. No more scribbling on pizza-stained character sheets. This is the legendary tome your hero carries into every dungeon — minus the weight and the curse.

> ⚔️ *"It's like a Bag of Holding, but for lore."* — Some Paladin, probably

---

## 🏰 What Is This Sorcery?

CYPHER LOG turns boring journal entries into **beautiful, browsable books** that float on your screen like a friendly wizard's familiar. When your players open it, they don't see a wall of text — they see a **library of leather-bound tomes** organized on wooden shelves.

Because let's face it: if it looks like homework, nobody's doing it.

### The Vibe:
- 📚 Books on shelves (organized by folders)
- 🎨 Custom cover images (judge a book by its cover — we dare you)
- 🏷️ Tags (for the obsessively organized)
- 🔒 Private logs per actor (your rogue's secret diary stays secret)
- 🌐 GM sees everything (all-seeing eye mode activated)

---

## 🎮 Player Mode: "This Is MY Book"

Each player sees only **their own actor's logs** — plus anything the GM has shared with the party. It's like everyone has their own private shelf in the guild library, but the GM has the master key.

> 🧙‍♂️ *"I cast Zone of Truth on the barbarian's journal."* — The GM, probably

**What players can do:**
- Write rich text logs with formatting, tables, and images
- Organize into folders ("Quests", "NPCs", "That One Tavern")
- Tag entries for quick searching
- Set cover images (make it look *legendary*)
- Share logs with the party (if they want to)

---

## 👁️ GM Mode: "I See All Your Secrets"

The GM gets a special shelf called **"PLAYER'S LOGS"** where they can browse everyone's journals. It's like having Detect Thoughts on the entire party, but socially acceptable.

**What GMs can do:**
- Everything players can do, but cooler
- See ALL player logs (for plot-related reasons, obviously)
- Create world folders that everyone sees
- Manage the library like the dungeon master of literature they are

> 🎲 *"The GM reads your journal. Roll for emotional damage."*

## 📝 Templates: Start Writing Faster

Don't stare at a blank page like it's a dragon you can't defeat. Pick a template:

- **Blank** — For the brave (or lazy)
- **Quest Log** — Track that fetch quest you're definitely finishing
- **Character Notes** — Remember why your character cares about anything
- **Session Recap** — Because nobody remembers session 3
- **NPC Codex** — "That guy with the thing at the place"
- **Inventory** — Catalog your increasingly absurd loot

---

## 🗂️ Folders: Organization for Adventurers

Create folders to sort your life:
- **"Main Quest"** — The thing you're supposed to be doing
- **"Side Quests"** — The thing you're actually doing
- **"NPCs"** — People you should remember but won't
- **"Lore"** — Backstory you'll never read again
- **"Tavern Stories"** — The only logs anyone actually writes

> 📁 *"I have a folder called 'Important' and another called 'Actually Important.' I'm not sure which is which."* — Every Player

## ⚙️ Settings: Make It Yours

Tweak everything:
- **Shelves** — How many rows of books (1-5)
- **Document size** — XX-Small to Large (for different reading glasses prescriptions)
- **Padding** — Space around text (for claustrophobic readers)
- **Autosave** — Every 5, 10, or 15 minutes (for the forgetful)
- **Icon position** — Move the launcher anywhere
- **Folder labels** — Flip text, change colors, adjust opacity

## 🐛 Known Issues (A.K.A. "Features")

- Players might spend more time decorating their logs than playing
- The GM now has *even more* evidence of what the players missed
- Your party will argue about folder naming conventions
- Someone will definitely write fanfiction in their journal
